01

The system under test

A pure-PyTorch reconstruction of XLSR-Mamba (Xiao & Das, 2024, arXiv:2411.10027) — a dual-column bidirectional Mamba state-space model built on a frozen wav2vec 2.0 (XLSR) front end, published to detect synthetic and voice-converted speech.

01
XLSR-2.0 backbone — 317.5M params, frozen throughout this work24-layer wav2vec 2.0 transformer pretrained on multilingual raw audio; extracts 1024-dim frame features at 16kHz / 320× downsampling (~62.5 frames/sec).
02
Front end — Linear(1024→144) + BatchNorm + SELUProjects backbone features down to the 144-dim space the Mamba head operates in.
03
Dual-column Mamba conformer — 1.79M params, the part we trainSix bidirectional Mamba layers run the sequence forward and backward in parallel columns, each pooled by learned attention, concatenated, and classified into bonafide vs. spoof.

Why the split matters later: because the backbone and front end never change, their output for a given clip is a fixed function of the waveform. Every clip's features can be computed once and cached — turning what would be hours of fine-tuning into single-digit minutes of head training. §5 relies on this.

02

ASVspoof5 baseline benchmark

Before touching the model, we established its true out-of-the-box accuracy on the benchmark it was built for — correcting two bugs in the original evaluation script that had been silently loading half the trained weights.

Bugs fixed before this number meant anything: the eval script hard-coded 6 Mamba layers per direction when the checkpoint holds 12, silently discarding 60 weight tensors while reporting "565/565 loaded OK" — and zero-padded short clips instead of repeat-tiling them, as the original training code does. Both are fixed; the script now hard-fails on any tensor mismatch instead of reporting a false pass.

A naive-threshold pitfall worth flagging: deciding "bonafide" at the raw p≥0.5 softmax cutoff gives only 86% accuracy on this benchmark — it catches nearly all spoofs but rejects most genuine speech, because the model's score distribution sits shifted negative. The EER threshold (−2.36) is the correct operating point and recovers 97.2% accuracy. The same trap reappears, worse, in §3.

The multilingual blind spot

The model above was trained on ASVspoof's English-centric, vocoder-focused attacks. Scored — unmodified — against modern Hindi/Bengali/Tamil TTS and voice-conversion deepfakes, it performs close to a coin flip:

Hindi is worse than chance — the model's scores are anti-correlated with the truth often enough to land above the 50% line. WhatsApp-style Opus compression barely moves any of these numbers (48.3% clean vs 46.1% compressed, overall), which says less about robustness and more about the fact that the model has no real signal to lose in the first place. This motivated everything that follows.

04

Building the fine-tuning corpus

Paired real and fake speech in three Indian languages, with a messaging-app compression pass baked directly into the data rather than left as an untested robustness claim.

SourceRoleDetail
IndicVoices-R (hi/bn/ta)real · label 1Studio-restored natural speech, 48kHz, speaker IDs
IndicSynth (hi/bn/ta)fake · label 0TTS & voice-conversion deepfakes (incl. FreeVC24), 24kHz

Every clip, resampled to 16kHz mono, then:

  1. Capped at 6 seconds, centre-cropped.
  2. Split speaker-disjoint, 80/10/10 train/dev/test — no speaker's voice appears in more than one split.
  3. Passed through a WhatsApp-equivalent codec: Opus, 16kHz mono, ~24kbps, VoIP mode, encoded and decoded back with PyAV/libopus — the same lossy round-trip a WhatsApp voice note takes. Training clips are stored as either clean or compressed (p=0.5); dev/test clips are stored as both, so accuracy can be measured per condition.

17,236 clips · ~2.9GB · train 12,730 / dev 1,904 / test 2,602

Scaled down from a 6GB target. HuggingFace's streaming API has no cheap resume — restarting mid-download re-streams and discards every already-seen row, which got expensive as cursors passed tens of thousands. Rather than pay repeated multi-hour re-catch-ups after transient stalls, the corpus was finalized at ~2.9GB. It was enough. The 12-language gated ai4bharat/IndicVoices set was also left out — gate approval didn't register for this account, and its audio format needs FFmpeg tooling not installed on this machine.

05

Fine-tuning method

Freeze everything except the 1.79M-param classification head, train it fast on cached features, and let a shrinking dev-set error rate decide when to stop.

SettingValue
Trainable params1.79M of 319.3M (0.56%)
OptimizerAdamW, lr 3e-4, cosine schedule
LossCross-entropy, class-balanced by construction
AugmentationRandom 100-frame crop, frame masking, feature noise
Early stoppingDev EER, patience 3
Batch size64 (VRAM-bound on 8GB laptop GPU)

Sequence length was cropped to 100 of the cached 208 frames per training step: the dual-column Mamba scan backprops through a strictly sequential Python loop over every timestep, so its cost scales close to linearly with length — the crop roughly doubled training throughput at negligible accuracy cost, since a WhatsApp/vocoder artifact is present in any 2-second window, not only a full 4-second one.

07

The forgetting cost

The fine-tuned checkpoint was re-scored on the exact ASVspoof5 dev subset from §2, to check whether it kept its original skill.

Before Indic fine-tune
2.77%
ASVspoof5 EER, same 9,000-clip subset
After Indic fine-tune
21.28%
~7.7× higher error rate

This is a textbook case of catastrophic forgetting: retraining only the 1.79M-param head on a new, narrower distribution (three languages, a handful of TTS/VC systems) pulled the classifier's decision boundary away from the broad ASVspoof attack taxonomy it was originally tuned against — even though the 317.5M-param backbone underneath never moved. The head is small and fast to adapt, which is exactly why it is also fast to overwrite.

This model is now a specialist, not a generalist. Deploy model_indic_ft.safetensors only where the traffic is genuinely Hindi/Bengali/Tamil deepfake screening. For a detector that needs to hold both skills, the standard fixes are joint training (mix ASVspoof and Indic batches in the same fine-tuning run), a replay buffer of original-task examples, or an elastic-weight-consolidation penalty on the head — none of which this run attempted.

08

Limitations & honest caveats

  • Small dev/test sets per language. Bengali dev is 454 clips, Tamil dev 258 — early-stopping decisions and the 0.00% Tamil EER numbers should be read as encouraging, not as statistically tight estimates.
  • Corpus scaled down from 6GB to ~2.9GB mid-build (see §4) after streaming-resume costs made repeated interruptions expensive. More data, particularly more distinct TTS/VC systems, would likely improve robustness to attacks not represented here.
  • Only 12 languages' worth of "fake" generators are FreeVC24 and related IndicSynth models. The fine-tuned head has not seen every deepfake technique in the wild — it has learned to catch the ones in this corpus extremely well.
  • Training crashed at epoch 5 on a CUDA out-of-memory error, likely triggered by a concurrent CPU-side evaluation job pushing the system over the edge while GPU VRAM was already pinned near its 8GB ceiling throughout. The epoch-4 checkpoint (already the best on dev EER) was kept as final rather than risk another crash chasing marginal gains.
  • Catastrophic forgetting on the original task (§7) is real and unmitigated in this run.
  • The gated 12-language ai4bharat/IndicVoices dataset was not used — access approval didn't register, and its audio needs FFmpeg tooling not present on this machine. This report covers 3 of India's 22 scheduled languages.
